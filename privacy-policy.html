Sukoshi privacy policy

Effective date: 21 November 2026

1. Who we are

Sukoshi is a journal app for Android. It is made by Dare 2 Thread Ltd, a company registered in England and Wales (company number 12931577), registered office 25 Beechcroft Avenue, Birmingham, England, B28 9ER, United Kingdom. In this policy, "we" and "us" means Dare 2 Thread Ltd.

Questions about privacy: sukoshijournal@gmail.com

2. The short version

  • You don't need an account. You never sign up for anything.
  • Everything you record in Sukoshi stays on your phone. We can't see it.
  • Sukoshi has no ads, and no analytics, crash reporting or tracking of its own. Google's Play Billing software inside Sukoshi sends Google technical records of purchase checks (see section 7).
  • If you buy Sukoshi Plus or leave a tip, Google Play handles the payment. We get a basic record of the sale, not your card details.

3. What Sukoshi stores on your phone

Sukoshi saves what you enter, in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • your medicine schedule, doses and the sites you note
  • weight, and waist and height if you choose to add them
  • symptoms, including any symptom names you add yourself, and notes
  • your weekly Hitoiki reflections: mood, what helped, and questions for your prescriber
  • how many doses you have left, if you use the supply reminder
  • reminder times and app settings, such as units, appearance and colour theme
  • weigh-ins brought in from Health Connect, if you turn it on, with the name of the app they came from (see section 8)
  • whether you have bought Sukoshi Plus and, if Google Play stops showing your purchase, the time Sukoshi first noticed. This is kept apart from your journal so that Plus is only switched off after two checks at least a day apart. Delete everything clears it.

Sukoshi also works things out from your entries on your phone, such as charts, percentage of starting weight lost and BMI. None of this is sent to us or anyone else.

Your data is protected by Android's built-in storage encryption. You can also turn on the app lock in Sukoshi. Sukoshi tells Android to leave its data out of cloud backup and out of phone-to-phone transfer. So your journal is not copied to your Google account, and Android's transfer doesn't move it when you set up a new phone. To move to a new phone, save a Data Passport and restore it there (see section 4).

We can't see your data. It never reaches us, so we can't read it, share it, sell it or recover it for you.

To delete your data, use Your data > Delete everything in the app, or uninstall Sukoshi.

  • Delete everything removes your whole journal from this phone: doses, weights and measurements, symptoms, notes, Hitoiki reflections, your supply count, reminders and settings. It also clears the home-screen widgets and gives back Sukoshi's Health Connect permission.
  • It keeps only the record that you bought Sukoshi Plus. The purchase belongs to your Google account, so you can use Restore purchase if you start again.
  • Uninstalling removes all of the app's data from your phone.
  • Neither one removes export or Data Passport files you have saved elsewhere.

4. Exports and backups you make

You can make a PDF, CSV or JSON export, or a Data Passport (an encrypted backup file). You choose where each file goes: your phone's storage, your own cloud storage, email, or another app.

  • We never receive these files.
  • Once you send a file somewhere, that service's own privacy terms apply.
  • A Data Passport is encrypted with a passphrase you choose. We never see the passphrase, and Sukoshi doesn't store it. If you lose it, nobody can open the Passport, including us.
  • Without the passphrase, a Data Passport shows only the date and time it was saved, how many entries it holds, the Sukoshi version, and the technical settings needed to unlock it. Everything you logged is encrypted.
  • PDF, CSV and JSON exports are not encrypted. Keep them somewhere safe.

5. Reminders

Reminders are scheduled on your phone and shown by Android. They are not sent through our servers or any push service.

Reminder notifications can appear on your lock screen, so they never show your medicine's name or dose. They use plain wording, such as "It's your usual day. Log it in Sukoshi when you're ready."

6. Home-screen widgets

Sukoshi's home-screen widgets are optional. Anyone who can see your home screen can see what a widget shows, so the widgets keep it short:

  • The Next widgets show the day and time of your next dose and, for a daily medicine, whether today's is logged. They never show your medicine's name or dose.
  • With Sukoshi Plus, the This week widget shows which days this week you've logged, the Weight widget shows your 7-day average weight and trend, and the Supply widget shows how many doses you have left.
  • The Weight widget has a Hide numbers option that shows the trend line only. It's on by default when app lock is on.

What the widgets show is kept in the app's private storage on your phone. It is never sent anywhere.

7. Buying Sukoshi Plus or leaving a tip

Sukoshi Plus is an optional one-off purchase made through Google Play. Google handles the payment under its own terms and the Google Privacy Policy. We never see your card or bank details.

You can also leave an optional tip (Say thanks in the app). A tip is a one-off payment through Google Play in the same way. It unlocks nothing, and Sukoshi keeps no record of it.

Google gives us a record of each sale. This includes the order number, the product, the price, the date, and your country, and your region or postcode where Google provides it. We use this to keep our accounts, meet tax rules and handle refunds. Our legal reason is that the law requires us to keep accounting records. We keep these records for 6 years after the end of the financial year they relate to.

Each time you open Sukoshi, it asks the Google Play app on your phone whether your Google account has bought Plus. Google Play answers using your Google account. Nothing from your journal is sent to Google or to us.

Google's Play Billing software, which Sukoshi needs for purchases, also sends Google technical records of its purchase checks: whether Google Play could be reached and how each check went, with basic details about your phone such as its model, Android version, country, language and mobile network. Google uses these records under the Google Privacy Policy. They never include anything from your journal, and we don't receive them.

8. Health Connect (only if you turn it on)

Health Connect is Android's own place for health data from apps such as your scale's app. If you turn it on in Sukoshi (Settings > Health Connect), Sukoshi can bring in your weight from it. It's off until you turn it on.

  • Sukoshi reads your weight and nothing else. It never writes anything to Health Connect.
  • Android asks you what Sukoshi can see. If you also allow past data, Sukoshi reads weigh-ins from before you connected, back to your starting date. If you don't, Health Connect only lets Sukoshi see the last 30 days.
  • Sukoshi reads only while it's open on your screen, never in the background.
  • Weigh-ins it brings in are stored only on your phone, like the rest of your journal. They're labelled "From Health Connect", with the name of the app they came from where Health Connect gives it. They go into a Data Passport or export only when you make one. They're never sent to us or anyone else, and never used for ads.
  • To stop, use Settings > Health Connect > Turn off, or remove Sukoshi in Android's Health Connect settings. When you turn it off in Sukoshi, you choose whether to keep the weigh-ins already brought in or remove them, and Sukoshi gives back its permission. Delete everything also gives back the permission.

The use of information received from Health Connect will adhere to the Health Connect Permissions Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

9. If you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address and what you write. We use it only to reply and to fix problems. We keep support emails for 12 months after the conversation ends, then delete them. Our legal reason is our legitimate interest in answering you.

Please don't send us your health details or screenshots of your journal. We don't need them to help you. If you do send them, we'll use them only to answer your question and delete them with the rest of the conversation.

10. What we don't do

  • No account, login or sign-up
  • No advertising and no advertising ID
  • No analytics, crash reporting or usage tracking of our own
  • No location, contacts, photos or microphone access
  • No selling or sharing of your journal with anyone
  • No AI or automated decisions about you

11. Children

Sukoshi is for adults aged 18 and over. It is not designed for or aimed at children. Because we hold no journal data, there is nothing on our side to delete if a child has used it. A parent can delete the data by uninstalling the app.

12. Your rights

Your journal is on your phone, so you can view, export, correct or delete it yourself at any time.

For the information we do hold (sales records and emails), UK data protection law gives you rights to see it, correct it, ask us to delete it, object to how we use it, and restrict how we use it. Email sukoshijournal@gmail.com and we'll reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113. We'd appreciate the chance to sort it out first.

13. Security

The journal is kept in the app's private storage, protected by Android's encryption and your optional app lock. Data Passport files use strong encryption with your passphrase. Sukoshi does not send journal data over the internet. The Google Play Billing records in section 7 travel to Google over an encrypted connection.

14. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. If a change affects what happens to your data, we'll also tell you in the app before it takes effect.
